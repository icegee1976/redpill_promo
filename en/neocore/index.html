Neocore · For VTuber Agencies, Virtual Production & Game Animation

No wearables, no markers. A few webcams, one PC, your whole cast.

Neocore turns a room, five or six 1080p webcams and a single RTX workstation into a multi-person motion capture stage. Performers wear whatever they walked in with. Set up the cameras, run the guided calibration, and start recording takes — as long as the cameras stay put, a saved project reloads with no recalibration.

02 · The recipe

One room, one workstation, your whole cast.

5–6 webcams + 1 RTX workstation = real-time mocap for 3–4 performers

Cameras
5–6× 1080p / 60 fps webcams (6 preferred), FOV 90°–110°, mounted at about 2 m with a slight downward tilt. Any reputable brand works; AverMedia PW315 is the officially recommended model.
Space
~5 × 5 m baseline for 3–4 performers. Officially tested up to 10 × 10 m with 8 performers (more cameras and compute required).
Workstation
Intel i7 13th gen or above, NVIDIA RTX 4070 or above, 32 GB RAM. One machine runs the whole system.
First launch
Automatic TensorRT tuning, 5–10 minutes, once. After that the system is ready on startup.

03 · Pipeline

From take to engine, three ways.

Record & export

Take Recorder captures your session and exports FBX — a standard animation format that drops into Unreal Engine, DCC tools, or any production pipeline.

Stream live

LiveLink streams performers into Unreal Engine 5 in real time. Subjects arrive on the UE5 Mannequin skeleton, ready to use in an Animation Blueprint. Also connects to CamVerse Studio, Zukunft Works’ own virtual production software.

Engines & VTuber apps

A Unity plugin (com.redpill.neocore) reads the Neocore motion stream inside Unity; a Warudo plugin covers VTuber workflows.

The straight answer on accuracy: Neocore’s precision is below optical motion capture — and the price is roughly one tenth of a high-end optical system. For live VTuber shows, virtual production and game animation, that is usually the right trade. When it isn’t, the comparison below will tell you.

04 · Honest comparison

Neocore does not win every row. Read this as a selection guide.

Criterion Optical (Vicon / OptiTrack class) Inertial (Xsens-class IMU suit) Neocore family (markerless RGB AI)
Absolute accuracy Sub-millimetre — best in class Joint angles excellent; absolute position drifts Centimetre-class — sufficient for most robotic learning tasks
Hands & fingers Limited — marker density and occlusion issues Requires add-on gloves 27-joint per hand, native (Neocore RT)
Subject burden Tight suit + markers IMU suit + cabling Street clothing; no wearables
Environment Studio-bound Indoor / outdoor / factory floor Indoor; depends on lighting and camera sightlines
Real-time latency Low (system-dependent) ~20 ms — best in class <250 ms end-to-end (Neocore RT teleoperation)
Capital + ongoing cost Highest — cameras, software, room build-out Mid to mid-high — suits, gloves, calibration Lowest — RGB cameras + workstation + software
Best fit Biomechanics research; cinematic VFX Teleoperation in unstructured or outdoor environments Multi-actor Physical AI datasets; VTubing, virtual production, game animation

Comparison reflects the method-by-method assessment in the Neocore RT Procurement & Integration Brief (June 2026). Rows marked Neocore RT describe the RT platform; the standard Neocore system shares the same markerless RGB approach. In the best-fit row, VTubing, virtual production and game animation describe the standard Neocore system’s positioning; the Brief’s own assessment covers the RT platform.
