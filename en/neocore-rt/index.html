Neocore RT · For Physical AI & Robotics Teams

The bottleneck in Physical AI is motion data. Collect it without suits.

Omdia counts roughly 13,000 humanoid robots shipped worldwide in 2025 and projects 2.6 million units a year by 2035. What limits that curve is no longer compute — it is how fast teams can feed real-world motion into imitation learning and teleoperation pipelines. Neocore RT captures that motion from standard RGB cameras, with operators in street clothes behaving like real workers, not suited-up rehearsers.

Robot training datasets

Multi-camera, multi-actor capture in a real assembly cell. The same gesture, captured across dozens of operators and sessions, at near-zero subject burden — the throughput that imitation learning is starved of.

Humanoid teleoperation

An operator in street clothing drives a humanoid in real time, as demonstrated at NVIDIA GTC Taipei 2026. The skeleton stream retargets to your robot’s kinematic model.

Six numbers that matter on a robotics floor.

<250 ms

03 · Choosing a capture method

An honest comparison of 2026 deployments.

Criterion Optical (marker) Inertial (IMU suit) Neocore RT (markerless AI)
Absolute accuracy Sub-millimetre — best in class Joint angles excellent; absolute position drifts Centimetre-class — sufficient for most robotic learning tasks
Hand & finger detail Limited — occlusion and marker density Requires add-on gloves 27-joint per hand, native
Setup & calibration Dedicated capture volume; markers + per-session calibration Suit donning, IMU calibration, per-session magnetic check Standard RGB cameras; minutes-class room calibration
Subject burden Tight suit + markers IMU suit + cabling Street clothes; no wearables
Environmental flexibility Studio-bound Indoor / outdoor / factory floor Indoor; lighting and camera sightline dependent
Real-time latency Low (system-dependent) ~20 ms — best in class <250 ms end-to-end
Capital + ongoing cost Highest — cameras, software, room build-out Mid to mid-high — suits, gloves, calibration Lowest — RGB cameras + edge box + software
Best fit Biomechanics research; cinematic VFX Teleoperation in unstructured environments Scalable Physical AI datasets; multi-actor industrial capture

Read this as a selection guide, not a leaderboard. Gait-pathology research still belongs on optical; a quadruped in a disaster site still belongs on IMU. Neocore RT is built for Physical AI training data in assembly cells, micro-factories and controlled industrial environments.

04 · Evaluation path

Five steps from question to deployment.

  1. Define the data requirement

    Imitation learning, teleoperation, or safety validation — precision and latency needs differ.

  2. Site survey

    Capture volume, lighting consistency, camera placement; lighting still matters.

  3. One-day pilot

    Your operators, your task. Deliverable: real skeleton data from your environment.

  4. Format validation

    Stream feeds Isaac Sim, ROS, MuJoCo or in-house pipelines; retargets to your robot’s kinematics.

  5. Procure & deploy

    Hardware reference (AAEON or equivalent), software licence, calibration support, capture cadence.
